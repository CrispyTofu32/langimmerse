Languages fade, one generation at a time

Among US Latinos, the share who say they speak Spanish well drops with each generation. Pew Research Center survey, 2011.

Immigrants91%
Their children82%
Their grandchildren47%

The same pattern shows in the home. Of Latino parents who say they speak Spanish to their children: 97% of immigrant parents, 71% of second-generation parents, 49% of third-generation parents (Pew, 2015 survey).

These figures are for Spanish and US Latinos, the best-measured case. A 2002 study of census data (Alba and colleagues, Demography) found Asian-American families shifting to English at about the pace European immigrant families once did, and Spanish speakers more slowly. Language loss is not a rule, though: the study found that family and community decide a lot. Pew 2011 ↗ Pew 2015 ↗ Alba et al. ↗

Before you chat

  • These are public rooms. Anyone using LangImmerse can read what you write, and nobody moderates them.
  • Never share your address, phone number, school, passwords or photos. Treat everyone as a stranger.
  • Names are not checked. Someone can pretend to be anyone.
  • "Block" hides a person for you only. It does not report them to anyone.
  • If you are under 18, use this with a parent or guardian who has read this.
